Industry 4.0

Industry 4.0 is the connection of physical production equipment to software that can collect, contextualise and act on its data. In practice it means three layers: sensors and controllers on the shop floor that already generate signals; a connectivity layer (OPC UA, MQTT, Modbus, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP) that carries those signals off the machine; and a platform layer — MES, IIoT, analytics — that turns them into OEE figures, quality records, maintenance triggers and schedules.

Nothing about it requires replacing your machines. Most of our projects start by instrumenting equipment that has been running for a decade.

Because most plants cannot answer basic questions quickly: which machine lost us the most output last week, why a batch failed, which tool is due for change, whether today's schedule is achievable. Without instrumentation those answers arrive weeks late, if at all.

Connected systems close that gap. They make losses visible while they are still happening, which is the only point at which someone can do something about them.

IoT is the general idea of connected devices. IIoT is the industrial subset — machines, controllers and sensors in production environments, where reliability, determinism and safety matter far more than in consumer applications.

Industry 4.0 is the broader operating model: IIoT connectivity plus MES, digital quality, traceability, simulation, robotics and the organisational changes needed to use them. IIoT is a component; Industry 4.0 is the programme.
