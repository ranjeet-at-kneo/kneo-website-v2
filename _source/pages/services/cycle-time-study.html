The problem

Most improvement budgets are spent on the wrong station.

Without measured data, the station that feels slow gets the investment — and the line output does not change, because the real constraint was three operations upstream.

A cycle time study replaces opinion with numbers: what each operation actually takes, where the waiting happens, and what the line could do if the constraint were removed.

The rule we apply: instrument, measure, then automate. Automating an unmeasured process usually produces a faster version of the same waste.

Our method

Industry-standard measurement, applied properly.

01

Time-motion study

Direct observation of each operation across multiple cycles and operators, separating repeatable work content from variation.

02

Process flow mapping

Mapping material and information flow through the line, including buffers, transport and inspection steps that rarely appear in the routing.

03

Bottleneck identification

Determining which operation genuinely constrains output — and how much of its cycle is value-added versus waiting, handling or rework.

04

Benchmarking against takt

Comparing current performance to optimal takt time derived from actual demand, so the target is grounded rather than aspirational.

05

Throughput gain calculation

Quantifying the potential output improvement from each intervention, so the investment case can be ranked by return.

06

Optimization recommendations

Root-cause analysis of delays with concrete recommendations — including PLC-based automation solutions where applicable.
