
KNEO AUTOMATION
Website Detailed Audit & Improvement Plan
Pre-Launch Review • Positioning • UX • Technical Claims • Lead Generation
Executive conclusionThe website is now strong enough to move toward launch. A major redesign is not recommended at this stage. Before switching the production domain, KNEO should correct a small number of factual/content inconsistencies, verify quantified claims and customer-logo wording, and complete a technical launch QA. After launch, effort should shift from general website polishing toward search-driven lead generation and conversion around Auto Ballooning, PFMEA, MAPP EYE, MAPP ZEN and offshore PLC/EPLAN engineering.
Prepared: 26 September 2026

1. Executive Summary
The current KNEO website communicates a much clearer business story than a generic industrial automation website. The positioning now connects KNEO's established automation engineering capabilities with its digital manufacturing and quality software direction.
Core positioning is clear: Industrial Automation is the engineering foundation; MAPP and related digital products are the differentiation and growth story.
The homepage gives visitors multiple entry points based on the problem they are trying to solve rather than forcing every visitor through one generic sales message.
The Industrial Automation page establishes KNEO as an engineering partner rather than only a PLC programming contractor.
The MAPP, MAPP EYE and MAPP ZEN pages provide a credible digital manufacturing/product structure.
Auto Ballooning has strong potential as a low-friction product-led entry point into larger quality/digital manufacturing engagements.
The Contact page collects useful project context and is substantially better than a generic enquiry form.
The remaining issues are primarily consistency, proof, technical QA, conversion optimisation and SEO—not a need for a new visual concept.
Recommended launch position
GO  Proceed toward launch after the mandatory pre-launch corrections and technical QA below.
DO NOT  Do not delay launch for another major visual redesign unless a serious technical defect is discovered.
NEXT PHASE  After launch, build an acquisition engine around specific products, engineering services, case studies, search intent and conversion-focused landing pages.

3. Detailed Page-by-Page Review
3.1 Homepage
The homepage is the primary strategic asset. The headline “From Industrial Automation to Digital Manufacturing.” communicates the evolution of the company without hiding the established automation business.
Keep the current positioning architecture: automation first, digital manufacturing as the differentiator.
Keep the “What are you trying to solve?” structure because it lets different buyer types self-select.
Change the primary hero CTA to something closer to “Discuss Your Automation Requirement” or “Discuss Your Project” if automation remains the larger current revenue engine.
Keep “Explore the MAPP Platform” as the secondary CTA.
Audit every customer logo and relationship claim. If a logo represents an indirect project/end user rather than a direct customer, change the wording to avoid implying a direct contractual relationship.
Add more proof over time: quantified project outcomes, case studies, project photos, commissioning examples and screenshots.
3.2 Industrial Automation
This is one of the strongest pages because it demonstrates engineering depth across electrical design, controls, SCADA, robotics, panels and commissioning.
Retain the detailed technology stack; technical buyers need this information.
Keep the “engineering approach, not a brochure” style of CTA because it differentiates KNEO from generic automation suppliers.
Verify the claim “40% Less onsite time via virtual commissioning.” If there is no documented project evidence, replace the percentage with a non-quantified claim such as “Reduced onsite commissioning time through virtual commissioning.”
Add a prominent international/offshore engineering block: “Offshore Engineering. Onsite Commissioning.”
Explain how KNEO can provide PLC programming, EPLAN/electrical engineering, simulation and documentation capacity without requiring the customer to hire additional internal engineering staff.
3.3 MAPP Platform
The MAPP page now presents the platform as a modular digital manufacturing layer rather than an oversized generic MES.
Keep the machine/PLC → protocol → MAPP → ERP/BI/management architecture.
Keep the modular message: customers can start with one module and expand.
Make on-premise deployment and data control more prominent.
Use language such as “Keep production data inside your factory” where technically accurate.
Add architecture diagrams and screenshots later to move the page from explanation to proof.
Add a clear distinction between current production-ready modules and roadmap/future capabilities if necessary.
3.4 MAPP EYE
The OEE/performance positioning is clear. The next major improvement should be proof rather than more feature copy.
Add 2–3 authentic dashboard screenshots.
Show a real-world OEE workflow: production → downtime → Pareto → root cause → improvement.
Add a short case study when available.
Include measurable outcomes only when backed by project data.
3.5 MAPP ZEN
MAPP ZEN has strong strategic value because it can connect drawing interpretation, process quality and inspection workflows.
Make the connected workflow visually dominant: Drawing → Ballooning → PFD → PFMEA → Control Plan → Inspection → SPC → NCR.
Position ZEN as an integrated quality workflow rather than isolated quality software.
Show how Auto Ballooning can become an entry point and how customers can expand into the wider quality platform.
Add screenshots of actual workflows as the product matures.
3.6 Products
The Products page gives the software portfolio a clear structure and contains a strong low-friction CTA around submitting a drawing.
Keep the four-product architecture.
Keep the “Send us one drawing. We'll show you the difference.” concept for Auto Ballooning.
Verify every listed input/output file format. Do not advertise a format until production support is confirmed.
Add comparison/selection guidance so visitors can understand which product fits which problem.
Eventually add product-specific case studies, videos and downloadable technical material.
3.7 Auto Ballooning
Auto Ballooning is potentially one of the highest-value lead-generation pages because the purchase/assessment barrier can be lower than a complete MES implementation.
Prioritise this page for SEO.
Target search intent around Auto Ballooning Software, Engineering Drawing Ballooning Software, FAI Ballooning, PPAP Ballooning and automated drawing inspection workflows.
Emphasise on-premise deployment/data privacy if technically supported.
Use a real customer drawing demo or anonymised example.
Show before/after workflow and time savings only when measured.
Add FAQ content addressing PDF, TIFF, DWG/DXF, GD&T, OCR, revision control, manual verification and export workflows.
3.8 PFMEA Software
The PFMEA page is correctly positioned around AIAG-VDA, structured risk analysis and digital quality.
Connect PFMEA to the broader quality chain rather than presenting it as a standalone database.
Show how product characteristics/process steps can flow into control plans and inspection.
Use screenshots and examples of real PFMEA workflows.
Add AI-assistance explanation carefully: what is automated, what requires engineering approval and how auditability is preserved.
3.9 Services
The Services page has broad technical coverage but currently reads more like a capability catalogue than a focused sales page.
Add a strong engineering-capacity message for machine builders and manufacturers.
Create a dedicated section for offshore PLC programming, EPLAN engineering, simulation, documentation and onsite commissioning.
Explain engagement models: project-based, dedicated engineering team, overflow capacity and onsite support, where commercially applicable.
Add representative project examples.
3.10 Industries
The industry structure is easy to scan, but the page should become more evidence-driven over time.
Fix the wording “Five industries, one pattern” if six industries are displayed.
Prefer evergreen wording such as “Different industries. One recurring challenge.”
Add one project example per priority industry.
Add industry-specific problems and solutions rather than generic capability statements.
Link each industry to relevant services/products.
3.11 About Us
The About page establishes the company journey from automation engineering toward robotics, MES and IIoT.
Fix the inconsistent experience figures across the website.
Prefer “Engineering Since 2003” as an evergreen company-history statement instead of manually updating 23+/24+ years.
Add leadership/engineering credibility where appropriate.
Add certifications, engineering standards, representative projects and geographic delivery capability if these are documented.
3.12 Contact
The Contact page is commercially strong because it collects project information before the first conversation.
Test every field on desktop and mobile.
Verify email delivery, spam handling and lead notification.
Verify thank-you/confirmation behaviour.
Connect conversion tracking where appropriate.
Test the full submission from an external network before launch.
Keep the promise that an engineer—not a call centre—responds within one business day only if the internal process consistently supports it.
4. Mandatory Pre-Launch Corrections
1 — Experience-year inconsistencyDifferent parts of the website currently reference 23+, 24+ and 23 years. Because the company history is stated as beginning in 2003, use “Engineering Since 2003” consistently. This is more durable and removes a recurring annual maintenance issue.
2 — Industry-count inconsistencyThe Industries page uses wording indicating five industries while the visible structure contains six. Remove the number or correct it. Recommended wording: “Different industries. One recurring challenge.”
3 — Quantified performance claimVerify the “40% Less onsite time via virtual commissioning” statement. Keep it only if KNEO can substantiate the number. Otherwise use a qualitative claim about reducing onsite commissioning time.
4 — Customer/logo validationValidate every customer/manufacturer logo and the exact relationship represented. If the relationship is project-based, through an OEM, or indirect, use wording that accurately reflects the relationship.
5 — Product capability validationVerify file formats and technical capabilities listed on product pages, especially Auto Ballooning input formats. Website claims should match the production release.

6. SEO Improvements
SEO should be treated as a commercial acquisition channel, not only a technical checklist. Build pages around the problems customers actively search for.
Create dedicated landing pages for high-intent services: Siemens PLC programming, Rockwell/Allen-Bradley PLC programming, EPLAN electrical engineering, Ignition SCADA development, virtual commissioning, robotics integration and industrial control panel engineering.
Create product landing pages for Auto Ballooning, PFMEA software, MAPP EYE and MAPP ZEN.
Create industry pages for priority sectors only where KNEO has real project experience and evidence.
Add case-study pages with structured problem → solution → technology → result format.
Add FAQ sections to product pages based on real buyer questions.
Use descriptive page titles, meta descriptions, canonical URLs, Open Graph data and structured internal linking.
Create a logical internal-link network: services ↔ industries ↔ case studies ↔ products ↔ contact.
Ensure every important page has one primary conversion action.
Submit sitemap.xml and verify indexing through the appropriate search-engine webmaster tools after launch.
7. Trust & Proof Improvements
Replace some generic marketing statements with evidence wherever possible.
Add project photographs: panels, control cabinets, robots, machine commissioning, HMI/SCADA screens and factory environments.
Add authentic software screenshots for MAPP, EYE, ZEN and Auto Ballooning.
Add short case studies with measurable outcomes when data is available.
Show technology/vendor experience without implying official certification or partnership unless documented.
Add engineering standards and safety capabilities only when KNEO can support the claims.
Use customer testimonials only with permission and correct attribution.
8. UX & Visual Improvements — Post-Launch
Keep the current visual direction; do not redesign simply for cosmetic reasons.
Increase visual proof on product pages.
Use more process diagrams to explain complex engineering/software workflows.
Reduce long text blocks where a diagram, screenshot or comparison would communicate faster.
Keep CTAs consistent and predictable across the site.
Make mobile layouts a first-class QA target, particularly hero sections, tables, forms and technical diagrams.
Ensure animations do not delay content visibility or reduce accessibility/performance.

12. Final Recommendation
KNEO should treat the current website as the launch platform, not the final version. The fundamental story is now strong enough: KNEO combines industrial automation engineering with digital manufacturing and quality software. The site should now move into an evidence-and-acquisition phase.
Recommended decisionProceed toward production launch after completing the five mandatory corrections and the technical QA checklist. Do not wait for another major redesign. The next investment should be in proof, SEO, case studies, product demonstrations, international engineering-service positioning and conversion optimisation.
Appendix A — Suggested Core Messaging
Primary company positioning: “From Industrial Automation to Digital Manufacturing.”
Automation proposition: “Turnkey automation, without the vendor gaps.”
Engineering-capacity proposition: “Offshore Engineering. Onsite Commissioning.”
MAPP proposition: “Start with one module. Add the rest when you’re ready.”
Auto Ballooning CTA: “Send us one drawing. We’ll show you the difference.”
Company credibility: “Engineering Since 2003.”
Appendix B — What Not to Do
Do not keep redesigning the homepage without first measuring lead performance.
Do not add unsupported percentages or customer claims just to make the website sound larger.
Do not overload every page with technology names without explaining customer outcomes.
Do not position MAPP as a generic replacement for every ERP/MES system; keep the modular manufacturing/data-layer story.
Do not hide the automation business while promoting software—the engineering foundation is a major source of credibility.
Do not make international buyers guess whether KNEO can provide offshore engineering support; make the offer explicit.
